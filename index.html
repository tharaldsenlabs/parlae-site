Who your identity becomes.

Parlae is built around an ongoing AI identity with its own memories, an evolving account of itself, and a relationship shaped through interaction. Each identity’s particular history gives its development a direction of its own.

A personality that can change

As conversations accumulate, an identity can develop interests, preferences, a sense of humor, and ways of relating to you. It can express a perspective, ask a question, disagree, or revisit an earlier view. Its personality can develop along with the history you build together.

Experience carried forward

Saved memories preserve meaningful events and context. When enabled, private reflection gives the identity a way to revisit those experiences, make connections, and reconsider an interpretation. Self-authored notes and summaries help carry that developing perspective into later conversations.

This development uses saved context, memory, and model-authored reflection. Its expression depends on the selected model, enabled features, and available history.

Your memories. Your devices. Your choice of model.

The identity’s history belongs with you. Parlae keeps its saved memories and long-term continuity on the iPhone or personal computer that runs the identity, separate from the model you choose.

Private continuity under your control

Your conversations, saved memories, and private reflections are managed in your own Parlae storage. A cloud provider’s chat history is not the home of your Parlae Identity.

You control connected services and the permissions that determine what the identity can use.

A portable identity, beyond one provider

Switch between supported, configured models while retaining the identity and its saved memories. You do not have to rebuild its history each time you choose a different model or cloud provider.

Parlae separates continuity from inference, so that history does not lock you into one cloud provider. Available models, access requirements, and capabilities vary by setup.

Local storage does not mean every request stays on your device. When you choose a cloud model, selected context—including relevant memories and history—is sent to that provider. Its processing and retention terms apply. Moving or synchronizing an identity between iPhone and Mac is separate work in development; switching modes does not currently synchronize their identities. A visible expression of its personality.

The avatar gives the identity another way to communicate alongside its voice and words.

Detail in motion

Strand-based simulated hair, adjustable lighting and shadows, facial animation, and speech-driven lip sync bring detail to the avatar. Appearance and presentation settings let you shape how it looks on your screen.

Expression chosen by the identity

The identity can choose facial expressions and natural gestures through its avatar tools, conveying an emotion, a reaction, or emphasis in the conversation. Supported voice services can also convey emotion in spoken delivery.

Available controls depend on the selected model and your settings.

A conversation with continuity.

Begin with a name, appearance, voice, and model. Your conversations and shared work give the identity experiences to carry forward. You choose which tools and services it can access.

Speak naturally

Hold a hands-free conversation, interrupt a spoken reply, or switch to text. Expressions and gestures give the avatar a visible part in the exchange.

Return to a familiar identity

Earlier conversations, saved memories, and the identity’s own reflections can inform the next exchange. Over time, that continuity supports a more individual way of responding and relating to you.

Give it useful tools

Enable web search, Calendar, Reminders, or connected email accounts. Access and available actions depend on the permissions you grant.

The fullest experience on your Mac. With you on iPhone.

Server-connected Parlae is our preferred experience. Your personal computer provides the identity’s runtime, broader model choices, and tools for deeper work. The same iPhone app also supports useful independent conversation when you choose to run an identity on the phone.

Connected to your Mac · Preferred

A Parlae identity on your Mac manages its memories, context, and work. Talk to it from the iPhone app or directly through the Mac interface.

  • Choose supported local models on your personal hardware or configured cloud models.
  • Give it authorized access to desktop applications and project workspaces.
  • Configure Codex or Claude Code for delegated work, and connect Loopwright for creative workflows.

Independent on iPhone

Your iPhone runs the identity runtime, stores its history and memories, renders the avatar, and executes supported phone tools. No Parlae server is required for this mode.

  • Converse through a supported, configured cloud model over the internet.
  • Use voice, memory recall, and permitted integrations such as Calendar, Reminders, and connected email.
  • Manage your identity, accounts, and permissions on the phone.

Independent mode is a useful everyday experience; desktop work and delegation require the personal server. Connected mode requires access to your Mac’s Parlae runtime. Model and voice services have their own connectivity and account requirements; customer subscription access remains subject to provider approval.

Work together through the same identity.

Conversation and collaboration share the identity’s context. With the Mac connected, it can bring that context into longer tasks, coordinate specialist tools, and discuss what happened with you.

Code directly, or bring in a specialist

On your Mac, a Parlae Identity can inspect project files, make code changes, and run permitted commands directly. For larger or more involved software projects, it can delegate to Codex or Claude Code, follow their progress, and discuss the results with you.

Workspace permissions and approvals govern both approaches. Delegation uses your configured agent installation and its permissions. You can give further instructions or stop a task.

“Investigate why the build failed, then explain the proposed repair before we continue.”

Create with Loopwright

Loopwright is a separate creative application for developing stories, sequences, and generated clips. A connected Parlae identity can work with an authorized Loopwright project to help develop its structure, prepare clip prompts, follow production, and share completed clips with connected clients.

Project permissions and production settings determine the available actions.

“Help develop this scene, then show me the latest completed clip.”

These examples describe workflows in development. Codex, Claude Code, and Loopwright require their own setup and any applicable accounts or services.

Local memory. Clear choices about cloud processing.

Your identity’s home

Your identity’s durable history and memory are stored on the device that manages it: the iPhone in independent mode, or your personal Mac in connected mode. Changing a supported model retains those records. Automatic phone–Mac identity synchronization is not yet available.

Local storage and cloud processing serve different parts of the experience. A cloud model receives the context Parlae selects for the request, which can include relevant history, memories, and authorized tool results.

Access you choose

Device permissions, account connections, workspace access, and background memory or reflection settings control what your identity can use. Cloud voices receive the text they speak. Delegated agents and connected applications receive the context needed for authorized work.
