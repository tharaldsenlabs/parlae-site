A conversation with continuity.

Parlae combines conversation, remembered context, and useful actions in one interface. Choose your identity’s name, appearance, voice, and model, then decide what it can access.

Speak naturally

Hold a hands-free conversation, interrupt a spoken reply, or switch to text. Expressions and gestures give the avatar a visible part in the exchange.

Build a shared history

Conversation history and saved memories support later conversations. Optional memory review and private reflections help the identity revisit earlier exchanges within your settings.

Give it useful tools

Enable web search, Calendar, Reminders, or connected email accounts. Access and available actions depend on the permissions you grant.

One iPhone app. An optional Mac connection.

Independent on iPhone

Your iPhone runs the identity runtime, manages its history and memory, renders the avatar, and executes supported device tools.

  • Cloud model requests go directly from the phone to the configured provider over the internet.
  • Speech, conversation, and tool permissions are managed on the phone.
  • Use the app away from your Mac with the connectivity required by your selected model and voice services.

Connected to your Mac

A Parlae identity on your Mac manages its context and work. Talk to it from the same iPhone app or directly through the Mac interface.

  • Give it authorized access to desktop applications and project workspaces.
  • Configure Codex or Claude Code to carry out delegated work.
  • Connect Loopwright for creative project and clip workflows.

Available integrations depend on your configuration, connected applications, and provider accounts. The Mac connection requires access to the Mac’s Parlae runtime.

Discuss the work. Follow its progress.

With the Mac connected, your identity can coordinate longer tasks while remaining available for conversation.

Delegate to Codex or Claude Code

Ask your identity to investigate a problem or work on a software project. It can delegate to a configured Codex or Claude Code installation, pass the task and permitted context, follow progress, and bring the results back into the conversation.

Workspace access, agent permissions, and required approvals govern the work. You can give further instructions or stop a task.

“Investigate why the build failed, then explain the proposed repair before we continue.”

Create with Loopwright

Loopwright is a separate creative application for developing stories, sequences, and generated clips. A connected Parlae identity can work with an authorized Loopwright project to help develop its structure, prepare clip prompts, follow production, and share completed clips with connected clients.

Project permissions and production settings determine the available actions.

“Help develop this scene, then show me the latest completed clip.”

These examples describe workflows in development. Codex, Claude Code, and Loopwright require their own setup and any applicable accounts or services.

Understand where your context goes.

Your identity’s home

In independent mode, identity, history, and memory are managed on the iPhone. In connected mode, the Mac manages the identity you are connected to.

Local storage and cloud processing serve different parts of the experience. A cloud model receives the context Parlae selects for the request, which can include relevant history, memories, and authorized tool results.

Access you choose

Device permissions, account connections, workspace access, and background memory or reflection settings control what your identity can use. Cloud voices receive the text they speak. Delegated agents and connected applications receive the context needed for authorized work.
